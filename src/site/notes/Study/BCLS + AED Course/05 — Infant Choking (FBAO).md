# Infant Choking (FBAO)

BCLS + AED Course — module 5. Verified against the SRFAC BCLS+AED Provider Course Manual (Rev 1/2022), Ch. 7. Infant = under 1 year old.

Complete airway obstruction kills within minutes if untreated — act while the infant is still conscious.

## Recognition

- Choking develops abruptly: coughing, gagging, or stridor (high-pitched noise on breathing in)
- If the infant also has fever/nasal congestion/hoarseness → suspect infection (epiglottitis/croup) → hospital, back blows may not help
- **Severe (complete) obstruction signs:** loss of voice, increasing breathing difficulty, face turning blue
- If the infant CAN cough → do NOT interfere, let them clear it

## Conscious infant — back blows + chest thrusts

1. **Position:** one hand holds the infant's **shoulder and rolls the infant toward you**; the other hand goes **between the legs to the back**, supporting the head
2. **Form the "sandwich":** hand on the shoulder, hold the **cheeks** (jaw) with the other hand, arms between the legs — infant supported as one unit
3. **Lift** the infant, step **one leg forward**, and **roll the infant face-down** resting along that front leg (head lower than body)
4. **5 back blows** — bottom hand supports; top hand (heel of palm) delivers **5 firm back blows between the shoulder blades**
5. **Sandwich and roll the infant over** to the other leg, now **face up** (head still lower than body)
6. **5 chest thrusts:**
   - Top hand: **ring finger at the armpit**, trace the **nipple line** to the centre of the chest
   - Bring **index and middle fingers down** beside it, **lift the ring finger up**
   - Deliver **5 chest thrusts** on the lower half of the sternum
7. **Repeat** the 5 back blows + 5 chest thrusts cycle until the object is expelled or the infant becomes unconscious
- Each blow/thrust should be firm, with the intent of expelling the object

## If the object comes out / is visible

- Place the infant **face-up on a firm flat surface**
- Support the head with one hand; **sweep the object out with your little finger (pinky)** — only if you can see it (no blind sweeps)

## Unconscious infant — SSCAV

- **S — Support.** Lower the infant onto a firm, flat surface (supine)
- **S — Shout.** Shout for help: call **995**, get an **AED**
- **C — Compression.** **30 chest compressions** — same technique as infant CPR (2-finger method on the lower sternum)
- **A — Airway.** Head-tilt + chin-lift; open the mouth and **check for the foreign object**
  - If **visible** → **sweep it out with the little finger (pinky)**, hooking along the inside of the cheek (never a blind sweep)
  - If **nothing visible** → repeat (C + A): another 30 compressions, re-check
- **V — Ventilate:** seal your mouth over the infant's **nose AND mouth**, 1 breath (~1 s), watch for chest rise; no rise → reposition head, re-attempt
- **Pulse check = brachial, NOT carotid** (inner upper arm) — for infants, always the brachial artery

## Rescue breathing (pulse present, no breathing) — infant rate

- **30 breaths per minute** (1 breath every 2 seconds)
- Give 1 breath (~1 s), then count **"2-a-thousand"**, then give the next breath
- Deliver **30 breaths in 1 minute**, then re-assess breathing and pulse
