# Essential of First Aid

> [!info] First Aid
> First aid is the **immediate care** given to someone who has been injured or suddenly ill, before professional medical help arrives.
> It involves **simple** but potentially **life-saving** techniques that can stabilize the casualty's condition, prevent the condition from worsening, and promote recovery.
> However, first aiders **must** ensure **their own safety**.

## The Importance of First Aid
- Most medical emergencies occur out-of-hospital
- When help is rendered, the casualty's chances of recovery significantly increase

## Benefits of First Aid
- **Save lives** — quick & appropriate response can prevent serious injury or death
- **Reduce recovery time** — proper initial care lessens the severity of an injury, helping shorten the recovery period and reduce long-term impact
- **Reduce pain & discomfort** — basic techniques, like cooling a burn or immobilizing fractures, help manage pain until professionals arrive
- **Improve confidence during emergencies** — training builds confidence and preparedness, enabling calm and effective response in critical situations
- **Create safer communities** — if more people are first aid trained, the overall community becomes safer and more resilient to accidents and health incidents

## Aims of First Aid
1. Preserve life
2. Prevent the condition from worsening
3. Promote recovery
4. Provide comfort and relief

## Roles & Limits as a First Aider
The primary role is to provide **immediate care** before help arrives. This may comprise incident management and performing basic first aid.

To do this well, first aiders should:
- Be familiar with locations, such as AEDs and first aid boxes
- Perform a basic risk assessment to ensure the area is safe and anticipate potential injuries
- Perform primary & secondary surveys to identify and prioritize injuries

However, the limits are:
- Consent is required to perform first aid — if the casualty is unconscious, implied consent applies
- First aiders do not **diagnose** — attempting to diagnose without proper medical knowledge may lead to incorrect assessment and potentially worsen the condition
- First aid is meant for **acute injuries or sudden illness**, not for handling complex trauma or injuries
- First aiders do not **administer medications**
- First aid is not for addressing emotional and psychological trauma

## Universal Precautions
Infections by pathogens may occur; minimize cross-contamination by:
- Covering open wounds with a bandage
- Using disposable gloves
- Practicing good hygiene
- Using PPE (personal protective equipment)

## Items in a First Aid Kit
1. First aid box
2. Eye pad
3. Gauze pads
4. Eye shield
5. Scissors & tweezers
6. Sterile water/solution
7. Disposable gloves
8. CPR shield
9. Band-aids
10. Triangular bandage (has high versatility)
11. First aid guide
12. Roller bandage

## Primary & Secondary Survey
### Scene Survey
- **Environmental conditions** — moving vehicles, fumes/poisonous gases, unsafe structures, trapped casualty
- **Hazards** — fire/smoke/faulty wires/water/etc., unseen dangers like toxic gases or falling objects
- **Mechanism of injury**
  - Fall from height → multiple fractures
  - Ejection from vehicle → multiple injuries
  - Sudden deceleration → blunt trauma

### Primary Survey
_A quick assessment of a casualty to identify and treat the most immediate threat(s) to their life._
1. Danger
2. Response
3. Shout for help
4. AED
5. Breathing
6. CPR

> [!NOTE]- For trained healthcare professionals
> 1. Rescue scene evaluation
> 2. Airway & C-spine
> 3. Breathing
> 4. Circulation
> 5. Disability — Neurological
> 6. Exposure & Environment

### Secondary Survey
_Performed after the primary survey is completed and when the casualty's life is not in any immediate danger. Information collected should be passed on to the paramedics taking over the care of the casualty._

- **History taking (SAMPLE)**
  - S — Signs & symptoms
  - A — Allergies
  - M — Medication
  - P — Past history
  - L — Last meal
  - E — Events/Environment
- **Vital signs**
  - Pulse rate
  - Breathing rate
  - Skin color — flushed, cyanosis, paleness, jaundice, bruising, clammy
  - Body temperature
  - Blood pressure
  - Blood oxygen saturation
- **Head-to-toe examination (DOTAS)**
  - D — Deformity
  - O — Open wounds
  - T — Tenderness
  - A — Abnormal sensation
  - S — Swelling
