# CPR and AED

## AED
An **Automated** External Defibrillator (AED) is a portable electronic device that detects life-threatening irregular heart rhythms and administers electric shocks (defibrillation) to help the heart start beating regularly.

Annually, more than 3000 people suffer from sudden cardiac arrest (SCA), with 80% of these cases occurring in public spaces. A common rhythm associated with SCA is ventricular fibrillation (VF), where the heart muscles do not contract effectively and disrupt normal blood flow to the organs. If normal heart rhythm and blood flow is not restored, the chance of survival falls.

## Safety Considerations
- No one should touch the casualty during delivery of the electrical shock. The AED will instruct everyone to stand clear, and the person operating the AED should repeat this command and ensure everyone is clear of the patient.

### AED Dangers (WMG — Water, Metal, Gases)
- **Water and metal** — both are good conductors of electricity, so you should not use an AED on wet or metallic surfaces. Electricity will take the path of least resistance; if there is water on the chest, the shock will travel through the water instead of the heart muscle.
- **Flammable gases** — do not use an AED in a room where there is a buildup of combustible gases. Sparks generated pose a risk of explosion and fire.

## Chest Preparation
- Expose the chest of the casualty adequately.
- Shave any excessive chest **hair** if it interferes with proper pad placement. A small razor is usually included in the AED kit.
- **Jewelry**, especially metallic jewelry such as necklaces and chains, should be moved away from the pads.
- For casualties with a **pacemaker** on the right side of the chest, apply the pads at least four fingers' breadth away from the device.
- **Medicated patches or monitoring electrodes** on the chest wall should be removed if they interfere with proper pad placement.
- Use a cloth to dry a wet or **sweaty** chest to ensure proper adhesion of the pads to the chest.

## Pad Placement
- Peel off the protective backing from the defibrillation pads.
- Apply the pads on the chest according to the instructions on the AED.
  - For infants or children, apply the pads anterior-posterior or anterior-anterior, making sure that the pads are not touching each other and are at least 2 cm apart.
  - For adults, apply one pad on the right side of the chest, just below the collarbone. Apply the other pad just below and to the left of the left nipple.
