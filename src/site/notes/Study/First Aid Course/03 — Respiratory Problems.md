# Respiratory Problems

## Human Respiratory System
The respiratory system's main job is to move fresh air into your body while removing waste gases. Once in the lungs, oxygen is moved into the bloodstream and carried throughout your body. At each cell in your body, oxygen is exchanged for a waste gas called carbon dioxide.

## Mechanism of Breathing
Involves a coordinated process of muscular movements and pressure changes within the chest cavity that allow air to move in and out of the lungs. This process is divided into two main phases: **inhalation** (inspiration) and **exhalation** (expiration).

1. **Inhalation**
   - Diaphragm contraction
   - Intercostal muscles
   - Increase in thoracic volume
   - Decrease in intrapulmonary pressure
   - Air flows into the lungs
2. **Gas exchange at the alveoli**
   - Oxygen diffusion
   - Carbon dioxide diffusion
3. **Exhalation**
   - Diaphragm relaxation
   - Intercostal muscle relaxation
   - Decrease in thoracic volume
   - Air flows out of the lungs

### Regulation of Breathing
Breathing is regulated by the respiratory centre in the brainstem, which ensures that breathing adapts to the body's oxygen and carbon dioxide needs.

- **Chemoreceptors** — located in the brainstem and blood vessels, these receptors detect levels of carbon dioxide, oxygen, and blood pH.
- **Increased carbon dioxide levels** — if carbon dioxide levels rise, the brain signals an increase in breathing rate and depth to expel more CO₂.
- **Voluntary control** — while breathing is usually involuntary, we can consciously control it to some extent (e.g. holding our breath, taking deep breaths).

## Respiratory Emergencies
These are acute conditions that impair breathing and require immediate medical attention. These emergencies can result from various causes, including obstructed airways, infections, trauma, or chronic conditions that suddenly worsen.

## Asthma
Asthma is a chronic respiratory condition where the airways become inflamed and narrowed, often due to allergens, exercise, or stress. An asthma attack is a sudden worsening of symptoms, including severe shortness of breath, wheezing, chest tightness, and coughing.

Assist the casualty to administer a quick-relief inhaler if available and seek medical assistance. If the person does not improve, call emergency services.

There are many asthma triggers:
- Dust mites
- Medicine
- Mold
- Pollen
- Pets
- Cold air
- Smoking
- Exercise
- Pollution
- Stress

**Signs & Symptoms:**
- Wheezing
- Cough
- Shortness of breath
- Difficulty breathing
- Pale, clammy skin
- Dyspnoea
- Chest tightness

### Metered-Dose Inhaler (MDI)
#### With Spacer (Face Mask)
![mdi-spacer-with-mask.png](/img/user/Study/First%20Aid%20Course/Images/mdi-spacer-with-mask.png)

1. Prime the MDI by shaking for 10 seconds
2. Remove the cap and spray 2 or 4 puffs into the air
3. Connect the MDI to the spacer, holding the MDI upright
4. Tilt the head back slightly
5. Place the mouthpiece between teeth and lips, or place the face mask over nose and mouth to form a seal
6. Actuate the MDI once
7. Take 5–10 deep, slow breaths
8. Wait 30 seconds before the next actuation

> [!NOTE]- Notes
> Flap movement on the spacer/mouthpiece indicates the number of breaths

## Hyperventilation
Hyperventilation is rapid or deep breathing, often due to anxiety, panic attacks, or certain medical conditions. It causes an excessive release of carbon dioxide, leading to symptoms like dizziness and tingling.

**Symptoms:**
Rapid breathing, tingling in the hands or feet, lightheadedness, and sometimes chest pain.

**Treatment:**
- Encourage slow, deep breathing to rebalance CO₂ levels
- Seek medical help if symptoms persist or worsen

## Fumes Inhalation
Normally accidental, casualties inhale chemicals through the nose or mouth, leading to intoxicating effects which may last a few minutes. There are cases where people, looking for a quick "high", repeatedly inhale gas fumes for an extended effect, leading to life-threatening situations.

**Symptoms:**
Headache, confusion, breathlessness, coughing, wheezing, skin discoloration, restlessness or excitement, unconscious.

**Treatment:**
Ensure the area is safe to enter — i.e. do not enter without proper equipment. Bring the casualty to a clear and open space, and assess for ABC.

## Anaphylaxis
Anaphylaxis (allergic reaction) is a severe, life-threatening allergic reaction that can cause swelling of the airways, leading to difficulty breathing. It may be triggered by allergens such as food, insect stings, or medications.

**Symptoms:**
Swelling of the face, lips, and throat, difficulty breathing, hives, and a rapid drop in blood pressure.

**Treatment:**
1. Administer epinephrine injection (if available)
2. Call emergency services immediately
3. Keep the person calm and in a comfortable position

### How to Use an EpiPen
1. Form a fist around the EpiPen and pull the **BLUE** safety cap off
2. Position the **ORANGE** end about 10 cm from the outer mid-thigh (either clothed or unclothed, avoiding seams and pocket areas)
3. SWING and JAB the **ORANGE** tip into the thigh at a 90° angle and hold in place for 10 seconds
4. Remove the EpiPen and massage the injection site for 10 seconds

## Pneumothorax
Pneumothorax occurs when air leaks into the space between the lung and the chest wall, causing the lung to collapse. It can result from trauma, certain medical conditions, or spontaneously without an obvious cause.

**Symptoms:**
- Sudden sharp chest pain
- Shortness of breath
- Sometimes a bluish tint to the skin due to lack of oxygen

**Treatment:**
1. Seek medical help
2. Position the person comfortably to make breathing easier
3. Cover the wound with an occlusive dressing

## Flu-Related Respiratory Distress
Severe influenza can cause respiratory distress, especially in young children, the elderly, and those with pre-existing conditions.

**Symptoms:**
- Severe shortness of breath
- High fever
- Chest pain
- Confusion

**Treatment:**
Immediate medical evaluation and treatment are necessary, as flu complications like pneumonia can be life-threatening.

## Croup (in Kids)
Croup is a viral infection causing inflammation of the upper airway, primarily affecting young children. It leads to a characteristic "barking" cough and difficulty breathing.

**Symptoms:**
Barking cough, noisy breathing (stridor), fever, and hoarse voice.

**Treatment:**
1. Keep the child calm and provide cool or moist air (such as from a humidifier)
2. Seek medical help if breathing becomes laboured or the child appears distressed
