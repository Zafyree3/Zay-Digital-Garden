# Foreign Body Airway Obstruction

An airway obstruction is a blockage of any part of the airway. An obstruction may partially or completely prevent air from getting to the lungs.

In a complete airway obstruction, a casualty is unable to **_speak_**, **_breathe_** or **_cough_** effectively and may turn cyanotic. This is an emergency that may lead to death if not treated immediately. The steps to manage airway obstruction for child and adult are as follows.

## Conscious Casualty with Mild / Partial Obstruction
- If a casualty has a partial airway obstruction:
  - They will be able to breathe, but with some difficulty.
  - There may be wheezing sounds as air passes through a narrower space.
  - They will be able to cough.
- First aid intervention:
  - Allow the casualty to clear their own airway by coughing.
  - Continuously check for deterioration, i.e. unable to produce sound, breathe, or cough.
  - Consider calling 995 for SCDF if repeated attempts are not successful.

## Conscious Casualty with Severe / Complete Obstruction
- If a casualty has a complete airway obstruction:
  - They will be unable to **speak**, **breathe** or **cough** effectively.
- First aid intervention:
  - Perform abdominal thrusts or chest thrusts (obese, pregnant, abdominal injuries/operations) until the foreign object is dislodged or the casualty turns unconscious.

## Unconscious Casualty with Airway Obstruction
- If the casualty turns unconscious during the abdominal or chest thrusts:
  - Support and position the casualty supine on a firm, hard surface.
  - Activate EMS — "Help! Call 995 for SCDF and get an AED."
  - Perform 30 chest compressions in the middle of the chest.
  - Open the airway and remove visible foreign objects.
  - Perform ventilation using the BVM to check for airway patency.
  - If the airway is patent, check for breathing and pulse.
