# Spinal Management and Application of C-Collars

## Spinal Management
In first aid, spinal management involves keeping a casualty with a suspected spinal injury still and stabilizing the casualty's head and neck. Spinal injuries are injuries to the spinal cord and can be caused by falling, diving, RTAs, or sporting accidents. Special care helps to prevent further damage to the spinal cord and reduce the risk of permanent paralysis.

### Signs and Symptoms
- Pressure in the head, neck, or back
- Intense back pain
- Paralysis, lack of coordination, or weakness in any part of the body
- Loss of sensation, numbness, or tingling in the hands, feet, fingers, or toes
- Twisted or oddly positioned neck or back
- Impaired breathing
- Loss of control of bladder or bowels
- Trouble walking or balancing

## Cervical Collar
Cervical collars or neck braces are used in first aid to immobilize the neck and support the head, minimizing further injury, particularly in suspected spinal injury.

### When to Use a C-Collar
- **Suspected spinal injuries** — if a person has trauma injuries, especially to the head, neck, or back, a collar can help prevent additional damage to the spine.
- **Falls, car accidents, or impact to the head** — situations with high-impact forces might cause spinal injuries.
- **Signs of spinal injury** — if the casualty has numbness, tingling, weakness, or loss of sensation in the limbs, they may have a spinal injury.

### Steps to Use a C-Collar
1. Ensure that the area is safe. Call for emergency services if you have not done so.
2. Keep the casualty's head still using heavy towels or rolled sheets to prevent unnecessary movement.
3. Maintain an open airway. If the casualty is unconscious and not breathing, begin CPR. If the casualty is breathing but shows signs of impaired breathing, gently align their head to a neutral position.
4. If you are trained, apply a cervical collar to minimize neck movement.

### Types of C-Collars
- **Soft collars** — typically made of felt/foam materials and cut to mould around the neck and jaw of the person to restrict motion. Since the collar is under the chin, it provides support and keeps the head in a normal position.
- **Rigid collars** — primarily constructed out of plastic materials, making them easier to clean. Rigid collars come in varying sizes and restrict motion in flexion and extension.
- Each cervical collar type has different effects on neck motion. Both rigid and soft cervical collars have been shown to limit neck motion in both directions, but rigid collars contributed to significantly more immobilization in all directions.

### Selecting the Collar Size
- Measure by holding your open hand on the patient's shoulders.
- Record how many fingers to the chin and transfer the measurement to the front of the collar.
